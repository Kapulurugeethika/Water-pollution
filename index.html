About water pollution

Water pollution is the contamination of water bodies such as rivers, lakes, ponds, groundwater and oceans by harmful materials. These materials include chemicals, sewage, plastic, oil and industrial waste.

Polluted water is unsafe to drink, harms fish and plants, and spoils the natural balance of the ecosystem. Because water moves, pollution released in one place can travel far, reaching farms, towns and the sea.

Less than 1% of the Earth's water is easily available as fresh water, so every drop we keep clean counts.

Causes of water pollution

Most water pollution comes from human activity. These are the five main sources.

Industrial waste

Factories release chemicals, heavy metals and hot water into rivers when wastewater is not treated properly.

Plastic waste

Bottles and bags thrown into drains and rivers end up in the sea, where they break into tiny pieces that never fully disappear.

Sewage water

Untreated household waste carries germs and bacteria into water that people may later use for drinking and washing.

Agricultural chemicals

Fertilisers and pesticides wash off fields during rain and flow into streams, lakes and groundwater.

Oil spills

Oil from ships, pipelines and drilling floats on the surface, blocks sunlight and coats animals that live in or near water.

Types of water pollution

Water pollution is grouped by where it happens and what causes it.

Surface water pollution

Affects rivers, lakes and ponds. It is the type we can most easily see, such as floating garbage, foam and dirty colour.

Groundwater pollution

Contaminants seep through soil into underground water. It is hard to detect and can take many years to clean.

Marine pollution

Plastic, oil and waste reach the oceans and harm corals, fish, turtles and sea birds.

Chemical pollution

Toxic substances such as pesticides, heavy metals and industrial chemicals poison water and build up in living things.

Effects of water pollution

Polluted water affects health, wildlife and the whole environment.

Human health problems

Unsafe water spreads diseases such as cholera, typhoid and diarrhoea, and long-term exposure to chemicals can damage organs.

Death of aquatic animals

Poisons, oil and low oxygen kill fish, turtles and other water life, and animals can also be trapped by plastic.

Environmental damage

Extra nutrients cause algae to take over lakes, harming plants and the food chain that depends on them.

Shortage of clean drinking water

As more sources become unusable, people must travel farther or pay more for safe water, and many have no choice but to drink unsafe water.

Prevention of Water Pollution

🚯 Avoid Plastic

Never throw plastic waste into rivers and lakes.

🏭 Treat Wastewater

Industries should treat wastewater before releasing it.

🌱 Reduce Chemicals

Use fertilizers and pesticides responsibly.

🌊 Keep Water Clean

Participate in cleaning rivers, lakes and ponds.

📢 Spread Awareness

Educate people about protecting water resources.
